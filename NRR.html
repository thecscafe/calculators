How To Calculate Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

Net Revenue Retention shows how much recurring revenue you kept and grew from the customers you already had at the start of a period. Revenue from new customers stays out of the number.

NRR = (Starting MRR + Expansion − Contraction − Churn) ÷ Starting MRR × 100

Worked Example

You start the quarter with $100,000 MRR from existing customers. They add $12,000 in upgrades, downgrade by $3,000, and $5,000 churns.

(100,000 + 12,000 − 3,000 − 5,000) ÷ 100,000 = 104% NRR. Your existing base grew 4% on its own.

What Is A Good NRR?

Above 100% means expansion from existing customers is larger than what you lose to churn and downgrades. Below 100% means the base shrinks unless new sales cover the gap. Companies selling to enterprises usually report higher NRR than those selling to small businesses, so compare yourself with companies in the same segment.

NRR vs GRR

Gross Revenue Retention (GRR) uses the same formula without expansion. GRR can never go above 100%, so it shows how well you hold on to revenue. NRR shows whether you also grow it. Report both: a high NRR with a weak GRR often means a few big upsells are hiding churn.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does NRR include new customers?

No. NRR only measures the customers you had at the start of the period. Revenue from new customers goes into total ARR growth.

How do you calculate annual NRR?

Take the recurring revenue from your customers 12 months ago, then measure what those same customers pay today, including upgrades, downgrades and churn. Divide today's figure by the starting figure and multiply by 100.

Is net revenue retention the same as net recurring revenue?

People sometimes mix the terms. Net recurring revenue usually means your total recurring revenue after churn, in dollars. Net revenue retention is a percentage that compares one group of customers over time.

What is the difference between NRR and NDR?

Net Dollar Retention (NDR) is another name for the same metric. Some companies say NDR, others say NRR.
