How To Calculate LTV For SaaS

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV or CLV) estimates how much revenue one customer brings in over the whole relationship. This calculator subtracts what it cost to acquire them, so you see the net value per customer.

Net LTV = (Average Revenue Per Period × Customer Lifespan In Periods) − CAC

Worked Example

A customer pays $800 per month, stays 36 months on average, and cost $6,000 to acquire.

($800 × 36) − $6,000 = $22,800 net LTV.

The Churn-Based LTV Formula

If you don't know average lifespan, estimate it from churn: lifespan = 1 ÷ monthly churn rate. At 2% monthly churn, the average customer stays about 50 months. Many finance teams also multiply revenue by gross margin to get a margin-based LTV:

LTV = (ARPA × Gross Margin %) ÷ Monthly Churn Rate

LTV To CAC Ratio

Compare LTV to what you spend to win a customer. A ratio around 3:1 is a widely used rule of thumb for a healthy SaaS model. Customer Success moves this ratio by extending lifespan and driving expansion. Use the CAC calculator to get the other half of the ratio.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good LTV for a SaaS company?

There is no single number. What matters is LTV compared with CAC. Many SaaS teams aim for LTV of at least three times CAC.

How does Customer Success increase LTV?

By reducing churn, which extends customer lifespan, and by driving expansion, which raises average revenue per customer. Both feed straight into the LTV formula.

Should LTV use revenue or gross margin?

Revenue-based LTV is simpler and fine for CS planning. Margin-based LTV is more accurate for finance and investor conversations because it reflects the cost of serving the customer.
